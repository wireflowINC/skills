# Carousel planner: a working guide

Use this to plan a 6-slide carousel for Instagram or LinkedIn. It is also the free deliverable when Wireflow is not connected: the slide sheet at the bottom is enough for anyone to build the set in a design tool.

## Why carousels work

A carousel earns attention twice. The cover has to stop the scroll on its own, and every swipe after that is a small yes from the reader. Instagram can show a carousel again with slide 2 first to people who skipped it, so slide 2 should make sense without the cover. Saves and shares count for a lot on both platforms, and a carousel that teaches one useful thing gets saved.

## The six-slide shape

| Slide | Job | What goes on it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | A headline that names the reader's problem or promises a result, plus a short "swipe" line |
| 2 | First point | The payoff or the most useful step, so a reader who sees only this slide still learns something |
| 3 | Second point | One idea, one headline, one short line |
| 4 | Third point | Same pattern; keep the numbering if the post is a list |
| 5 | Fourth point or proof | A last step, a before and after, or one number that backs the claim |
| 6 | Call to action | One ask only: save, follow, comment a word, or visit the link in bio |

A list post ("4 ways to...") fits slides 2 to 5 exactly. A story post uses them as before, turning point, after and lesson.

## Copy rules

- Headline: 3 to 7 words. It must make sense alone, because many people read only the headlines.
- Body line: 12 words or fewer. Cut every word that does not change the meaning.
- One idea per slide. If a slide needs two headlines, it is two slides.
- Plain words, no hashtags, no emojis inside the design, no long numbers.
- Only true claims. No made-up statistics, results, awards or quotes.
- Every word that should appear on a slide goes inside quotation marks in the slide text. Words outside quotes are design direction and are never written on the image. This is how the app keeps stray words, colour codes and labels off the slides.

## The brand style block

One short paragraph, shared by all six slides:

1. **Background**: a colour name, with a hex code if they have one ("warm off-white (#FAF6F0)").
2. **Text colour** and **one accent colour** for icons, underlines and numbers.
3. **Type**: the feel of the headline font ("bold geometric sans serif", "heavy condensed sans", "classic serif").
4. **Illustration style**: flat line icons, simple flat illustrations, or soft 3D shapes. Keep it to one style.
5. **Footer**: the handle in quotes ("@yourbrand") and where it sits, plus where the slide number goes.

Strong contrast reads best on a phone: dark text on a light background, or light text on a dark one. Avoid mid-tone text on a mid-tone background.

## Writing one slide for the app

Each slide text is one short paragraph:

- The headline in quotes: `Headline: "Lead with the payoff".`
- Any body line or small line in quotes: `Body text: "Say what they get before you explain how."`
- The visual, outside quotes: `Visual: a small arrow icon.`
- The slide number in quotes: `Slide number in the footer: "2/6".`

Slide 1 also says it is the cover. Slide 6 says it is the call to action.

## How the set stays consistent

The app draws slide 1 from the brand style and its text alone. Slides 2 to 6 are made by editing slide 1: the background, colours, fonts, margins and footer stay, and only the words, the icon and the slide number change. So the cover sets the look for the whole carousel; if the cover's style is wrong, fix the brand style and run again rather than patching single slides.

## Sizes and posting

- The app renders portrait 3:4 slides. Instagram shows carousels at up to 4:5, so the top and bottom edges may be trimmed a little; the app keeps every word inside the 4:5 area to allow for that. Use the same crop on every slide.
- Instagram: up to 20 images per carousel, all at the same shape. Post the six in order.
- LinkedIn: a carousel is a document post. Put the six images into one PDF in order (most design tools and phone photo apps can export a PDF), then upload it as a document with a short title.
- Alt text: write one plain sentence per slide saying what it shows and repeating its headline.
