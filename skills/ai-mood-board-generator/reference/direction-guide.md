# Direction guide

Use this to shape the app inputs, and as the free deliverable when Wireflow is not connected.

## 1. A brief that gives the app something to work with

Three sentences are enough:

- **Who:** the client, in concrete terms ("family-run roaster in a harbour town"), not the category ("premium coffee brand").
- **Why now:** the moment that needs a look (a launch, a rebrand, a first city cafe).
- **For whom:** the audience and what they are tired of. A tension gives the direction an edge.

Leave out media plans, budgets and deliverables. They do nothing for the picture.

## 2. Mood words

Pick three to six that a photographer could act on.

| Weak | Stronger |
|---|---|
| premium | quiet, heavy paper, lots of air |
| modern | hard shadows, flat colour, one shape |
| fun | loud, clashing, slightly wrong on purpose |
| authentic | weathered, handmade, a little uneven |
| clean | pale, matte, unhurried |

## 3. Reference notes

The app reads words, so describe what you would otherwise pin to a wall:

- An era and a medium: 1960s travel posters, 1990s disposable camera flash, riso print.
- A place and a time of day: harbour at first light, motorway diner at 2am.
- Materials: enamel, rough linen, sun-faded paper, wet concrete.
- One clear "avoid": it often says more than the rest.

Rewrite names as descriptions. "A Wes Anderson look" becomes "centred symmetrical framing, pastel sets, flat front light".

## 4. Making routes differ

Three routes that vary only in colour are one route. Change one of these:

| Lever | Route A | Route B |
|---|---|---|
| World | the harbour the brand comes from | the city it is arriving in |
| Era | 1960s optimism | present-day documentary |
| Medium | photographed | printed or illustrated |
| Attitude | warm and close | dry and funny |

Give each route a short name; clients remember "Salt" and "First Light", not "Option B".

## 5. Free written direction (no connector)

Fill this in per route and hand it to a designer, or paste each prompt into any image tool:

```text
Route: <short name>
Idea: <one line on the look>
Palette: <four or five named colours with hex codes>
Tone: <three or four words>
Avoid: <what would break it>

Image 1, hero: <subject, composition, medium, light, colours>
Image 2, texture: <close-up of a material from this world, same medium and light>
Image 3, place: <setting and atmosphere, same medium and light>
Image 4, detail: <hands, gesture or object in use, no recognisable face>
```

Repeat the same medium, palette and light in all four prompts; that repetition is what holds a set together when you generate them one at a time. Keep every picture free of words and logos.
