# Thumbnail concepts: a working guide

Use this to plan three thumbnail concepts for one video. It is also the free deliverable when Wireflow is not connected: hand over the concept sheet and anyone can build it in a design tool.

## The job of a thumbnail

A viewer decides in about a second, often on a small phone screen, next to a dozen other videos. A thumbnail has to answer one question fast: "why should I click this one?" The title and the thumbnail work as a pair: the thumbnail should add something the title does not say, not repeat it word for word.

## Headline rules

- 2 to 5 words. Fewer is better. At most two lines.
- Never copy the video title. Add tension, a result or a question instead ("I tried cold plunging for 30 days" becomes "DAY 30 SHOCKED ME").
- Plain words people can read at a glance. No hashtags, no emojis, no long numbers.
- Only true promises. If the video does not show it, the thumbnail does not claim it.
- The same headline goes on all three concepts in one run, so the test compares visuals, not words. Test a second headline in a second run.

## Seven concept angles

Pick three different ones per video, not one idea reworded three times.

| Angle | What the image shows | Works for |
|---|---|---|
| Reaction face | A close-up face with a strong, honest emotion next to the subject | Challenges, reviews, vlogs |
| Before and after | A split frame, left the start, right the result, an arrow between | Transformations, tutorials, fitness, makeovers |
| The object | One hero object, big and sharp, on a clean background | Product reviews, unboxings, tech |
| Circle the detail | A real scene with a red circle or arrow on the one thing that matters | Explainers, mysteries, "you missed this" |
| Versus | Two things side by side with a clear contrast in color | Comparisons, "X vs Y" |
| Big number | One number as the visual hook (a price, a day count, a ranking) | Money, lists, experiments |
| Scene at a moment | The most dramatic moment of the video frozen in place | Travel, stories, builds |

## Writing one concept for the app

Each concept is one paragraph with five parts:

1. **Subject**: who or what, described plainly ("a man in his 30s", "a matte black espresso machine").
2. **Emotion or action**: what the subject is doing or feeling.
3. **Setting and light**: where, and the light that sets the mood (cold blue, warm sunrise, studio).
4. **Colors**: two or three strong colors that contrast; the background calmer than the subject.
5. **Headline placement and style**: where the words go (left third, top band) and how they look (heavy white letters with a black outline, bold yellow).

Keep the bottom right corner clear: YouTube puts the video length badge there.

## Layouts that read at small size

- Subject on one side, headline on the other (rule of thirds).
- Big headline on a solid color band at the top, image below.
- No more than three elements: subject, headline, one accent (arrow, circle or icon).
- High contrast between letters and background: white on dark, black on yellow.

## Testing

YouTube Studio's Test and Compare lets a channel try up to three thumbnails on one video. Upload the three concepts, let the test run, keep the winner. Then try a new headline on the winning concept.

## Sizes

- YouTube video thumbnail: 16:9, at least 1280x720, under 2 MB. The app renders 2560x1440; export a JPG under 2 MB if the PNG is larger.
- Shorts: the app is 16:9 only. For a vertical cover, plan the concept here and build it in a design tool, or crop the centre of the 16:9 image only if the subject and headline sit there.
