# Featured image guide

Use this to prepare the app inputs, and as the free deliverable when Wireflow is not connected.

## 1. The style note (write once per blog)

The style note is what makes post 40 look like post 1. Write it once, keep it word for word, and paste it into every run.

Cover four things in one or two sentences:

| Part | Examples |
|---|---|
| Medium | flat editorial illustration, soft 3D clay, paper cut-out, isometric line art, moody product photo, risograph print |
| Palette | three colours, hex codes if the brand has them: background, main, accent |
| Mood | calm and friendly, bold and technical, warm and human, premium and quiet |
| Density | "one object, plenty of empty space" reads best at thumbnail size |

Ready-made notes:

- **SaaS / productivity:** Flat editorial illustration with soft grain, warm cream background, deep navy and coral accents, simple geometric shapes, plenty of negative space.
- **Friendly team or HR blog:** Soft 3D clay illustration, rounded shapes, pastel sage and warm peach with a deep teal accent, gentle studio light, plenty of empty space.
- **Developer blog:** Isometric line art in white on a near-black background, one electric green accent, crisp and technical, lots of dark space.
- **Food, travel, lifestyle:** Natural-light editorial photo, shallow depth of field, warm earthy tones, one subject on a simple surface, uncluttered.

## 2. The summary

The app's concept step reads the summary, so write it for the picture, not for SEO:

- Lead with the reader's payoff ("a three-question Friday review replaced two status meetings").
- Name one concrete thing that could be drawn (a clipboard, a calendar, an inbox, a bridge).
- Leave out brand names, people and numbers you do not want to appear as objects.
- Two to four sentences. Longer does not help.

## 3. Concept patterns

When you brief a designer, or want to check that the app picked a sensible idea, these patterns work for blog headers:

| Pattern | When | Example |
|---|---|---|
| The one object | How-to and process posts | a single clipboard with a check mark |
| Before and after | Results and case studies | a tangled cable on the left, a neat coil on the right |
| The metaphor | Opinion and strategy posts | a small boat with a big sail for "small team, big reach" |
| The scene | Stories and interviews | an empty desk by a window at sunrise |
| The system | Explainers and frameworks | three linked shapes in a loop |

Avoid in all of them: screens with writing, charts with fake numbers, stock-photo handshakes, letters and logos.

## 4. Sizes per platform

Both app outputs are 16:9. The share card keeps its title inside the centred band, so these crops all work:

| Where | Size | Which file |
|---|---|---|
| WordPress / Ghost / Webflow featured image | theme dependent, usually 16:9 or 3:2 | hero |
| Open Graph (`og:image`), Facebook, LinkedIn, Slack | 1200x630 (1.91:1) | share card |
| X (`twitter:image`, large card) | 1200x628 | share card |
| Newsletter header | 600 to 1200 wide | hero |

Many CMSs and SEO plugins (Yoast, Rank Math, Ghost) have a separate social image field: put the share card there and the hero as the featured image.

## 5. Free concept brief (no connector)

Fill this in per post and hand it to a designer:

```text
Post: <title>
Style note: <the blog's style note, word for word>
Concept: <one pattern from section 3, one sentence on the subject, setting, light>
Hero: 16:9, no text, subject off-centre with empty space on one side
Share card: same picture, subject moved aside, title in bold type,
            kept inside 1200x630 (clear of the top and bottom 8%)
```
